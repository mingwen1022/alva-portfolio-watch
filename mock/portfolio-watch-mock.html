  • Fixed sample portfolio, seeded random series. No data layer, no engine. mock device
  • Alert order is computed: priority arrives on each finding as severity × position weight × novelty and the list is a read of it. Which finding leads a merged card is decided by source — a line you set outranks a signal we raised — then by the earlier knownAt. output-schema §3 findings[].priority · lead by source, then knownAt
  • Dedup / suppression / cooldown not implemented. The high-volatility PV1 downgrade is not implemented. registry §6.4 dedup · signal-spec PV1 downgrade rule ①
  • Every news tier appears at once so they can be compared. A real day shows one tier per alert. mock device · tiers defined in signal-spec EV6
  • The timing tag is arithmetic, not a verdict. before / after / none come from comparing each source's publishedAt against the instant the price moved — for a daily rule the session's largest 15-minute bar, for an intraday rule the trigger bar itself. It is not the trigger: a daily rule confirms at the close, and "published before 16:05" would make every headline filed all day count as early. Nothing here asks the model, and the page recomputes the tag rather than printing the one the pipeline wrote. after is a tier, not a failure — it keeps the same paragraph and the same source list as before. signal-spec §5.7 ② · output-schema §九 L3
  • Two different news filters run on this page, on purpose. The material under an explanation comes through EV6's strict chain (relevance = 1.0, event topic, ±120 minutes around the move) because attaching the wrong story asserts something false. The "related news" blocks on Tab 1 and Tab 2 are a display list that asserts nothing, so they run wide — relevance ≥ 0.80, no topic gate, the whole day, most recent first. The strict chain left 84% of non-trigger days empty. So the two lists on one page can hold different numbers of items about the same symbol; that is the design, not a bug. The counts on this page are the run's own. content-spec ⑥ · signal-spec EV6
  • Crypto alerts carry no attribution block. The endpoint does cover crypto symbols — symbol=BTC returns results — but across 25 tokens and 811 intraday triggers, every combination of six relevance thresholds, two windows and outlet-count gates measured below 1.0 (0.29 at the three-outlet gate). They fall short of the relevance = 1.0 gate EV6 applies, so the block stays empty. Loosening that gate would reach crypto, and would require re-measuring the false-attach rate. signal-spec EV6, the relevance = 1.0 gate
  • The sample PV5 alert fires at 14:20, so this page does not show the opening-bar case, where the volume filter removes almost nothing (the volume condition is already met on 88.6% of price-condition bars at 09:30, against 54.2% across all bars). signal-spec PV5, opening-bar filter rate 88.6%
  • Every news row is a real item with a real destination: href comes from the endpoint's url field, with target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer", and the whole row is the target rather than the headline alone. banner_image is deliberately unused: third-party imagery would turn an alert into a content feed. Five of the eight holdings returned nothing this run; those say so rather than showing a shorter list. link contract in content-spec ⑥
  • The Market tab has no forward macro calendar cell. The platform has no such endpoint — macro/economic-calendar returns 404 — so "there is an FOMC today" cannot be built at all. content-spec §6, macro/economic-calendar 404
  • The quiet-day state is a different day, not this one with the cards removed: price and volume readings are redrawn under both lines for every holding, and the day P/L figures follow them. Share counts, prices and weights are the same book. mock device
  • Intraday trigger counts are a marking device, not a measurement. The two-year chart carries no 15-minute track — building one for 502 sessions would be inventing data — so days are flagged as "had an intraday trigger" by position in the daily hit list, and no intraday threshold is evaluated. Both places that show them are affected: the counts in the scan table ("14 daily · 14 intraday") and the blue dots on the chart. Under the real rule the ratio would differ by asset class, since the intraday price threshold is 4.75 sigma on US names and 10.0 on crypto; here it does not. mock device · real thresholds in signal-spec §2, PV5 θz 4.75 US / 10.0 crypto
  • The scan table shows the close, so the intraday form of its volume column (1.1× as of 14:30) is not on screen. That reading is partial by construction — volume so far today over a median of whole days — and is labelled rather than scaled up, since scaling needs an intraday volume curve we have not measured. output-schema §3 scan[].volume.partial · asOfSession
  • “Below its own high” is a drawdown on portfolio value, not a return on cost. Two things the spec has not settled: what window the high is taken over, and whether deposits are stripped out before it is computed. output-schema §10, window and cash-flow handling still open
  • Portfolio-level Total P/L is the sum of open positions only — closed trades and cash flows are out of scope here. output-schema §10 portfolio.totalPL
  • 0700.HK is priced off a θv of 2.0, the US equity value. Its "unvalidated thresholds" mark is correct, but under the fallback rule an asset class with at least 12 available symbols gets its own θv solved at pool level — scanning 1.0 to 6.0 for the value whose trigger-day share lands nearest 4.16% — and the screener covers Hong Kong. The real number would be solved, not borrowed. signal-spec §2, fallback branch ① (≥ 12 symbols)
  • One design-linter error is left standing on purpose. alva lint playbook reports one error on this file. The error is single-scroll-container: the contract allows only body and html to scroll, and the alerts rail scrolls inside itself. Everything else the linter found — 21 element-component bindings, 9 font weights outside the 400/500 the design system defines, 1 weight over the 24px size limit — has been fixed rather than argued with. This one is kept because the rail is the only layout measured to leave no dead space beside the cards, and its scroll is what makes it a rail. The warnings are all canonical-class overrides on .on and .open — one per local switch — each a deliberate style, and their count moves with the number of switches rather than with anything being wrong. A count written here would go stale the next time one is added, which is what happened to this sentence. design-contract.yaml global.scroll.sole-scroll-container
